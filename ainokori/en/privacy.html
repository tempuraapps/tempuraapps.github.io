AINOKORI Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 11, 2026

About the app · Support · 日本語

AINOKORI (“the App”) is provided by TempuraApps (“we”). The App consists of an app for iPhone and Apple Watch, together with a companion app for Mac. This policy explains how we handle user information.

1. Information the Mac app reads

The Mac app reads only usage records from among the files that the AI coding tools installed on your Mac (Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI) save to your home folder for their own operation. Specifically: tokens and requests consumed, the usage rate for each usage window, the reset time for each usage window, and whether each tool is installed. GitHub Copilot is not covered in the current version (nothing is extracted).

What’s read is converted, inside the Mac app, into numbers showing which tool is currently used by how much, and only those numbers are synced as described in section 3. The content of the original files it reads is never stored or transmitted.

If you use Claude Code and add the one-line status-line setting shown in the Mac app, Claude Code writes its 5-hour and weekly usage rates and their reset times into the Mac app’s storage. The Mac app reads only those four numbers, plus when they were written and whether Claude Code has responded, and never reads or writes Claude Code’s settings file (~/.claude/settings.json).

2. What the Mac app never extracts, stores or sends

Only the usage numbers are extracted; conversations, code, credentials, project names and log paths are never stored or sent. For conversation text and code, the program has no data type that could read them. To find usage records it lists folders, but the folder names and file paths it finds there are never stored or sent. The following are kept in the app’s settings on this Mac: the location of each folder you allow (its path and bookmark, to keep your permission), and, to avoid duplicate identifiers after migrating to a new Mac, the Mac’s model and name and a one-way value derived from its hardware identifier. The folder locations and the one-way value are never sent to iCloud or any server (the Mac’s model and name are also among the items synced in section 3). None of the following is extracted:

  • The text of your conversations with AI — prompts you sent, responses you received
  • Source code, file contents, diffs
  • Credentials, API keys, access tokens (the App never opens any tool’s credential files)
  • Project names, folder or file paths found in the logs, Git branch names
  • Shell history, environment variables, SSH keys

The Mac app also collects no analytics. The only service it talks to is your own iCloud; it never connects to our servers or to any AI provider’s API. Data reaches your iPhone only through iCloud, as described in section 3.

3. Syncing via iCloud

The usage numbers the Mac app creates are stored in the private iCloud database of your own Apple Account, and read by the iPhone signed in with that same Apple Account. Your Apple Watch receives the values directly from your iPhone. We cannot access this database. We have no servers, and no account registration is required.

What’s synced is limited to: the AI tool type, the usage window type, the usage rate, the reset time, the window’s start time, tokens and requests consumed and their limit (including an estimate, or the preset for the plan or daily limit you chose), how certain each value is and what it is based on, recent readings (time and each window’s values), when the data was read, whether the data could be read and why not, the version of the reading method, a random per-Mac identifier, and your Mac’s name, model, app version, polling interval and last-response time. If you don’t use iCloud, your Mac’s data never reaches your iPhone (the App itself can still be used to the extent of, for example, viewing sample data). The values in iCloud are overwritten by your Mac with each update and remain until you delete them as described in section 10.

4. Information stored on iPhone and Apple Watch

The usage history you receive, your notification settings and your display settings are stored on your device. History values are never sent to our servers or a third party’s. When you change a notification or display setting, only which setting was changed and which option was chosen (the notification type and on/off, the chosen thresholds, remaining-vs-used display, and the result of the first notification-permission prompt) is included in the analytics described in section 7. History is kept on your device for the most recent 90 days; anything older is erased automatically.

5. Location data

The App does not use location data. It never requests or collects your location.

6. Notifications

Notifications from the App are local notifications assembled entirely on your iPhone. You’re notified when your capacity runs low, when you hit a limit, when a usage window resets, and at the time of your daily report. Notification content never passes through our servers or a third party’s. You can change notification permission at any time in your device’s Settings.

To let your iPhone know a Mac update is ready, the App also uses Apple’s iCloud notification mechanism — a silent notification that never appears on screen. It carries no usage numbers; it’s only a trigger for your iPhone to fetch the latest values from iCloud.

7. Usage analytics (PostHog)

To improve quality and features, the iPhone app measures anonymous usage with the analytics service PostHog. What’s sent is limited to purchase-related actions — opening the Pro screen (and from where), the purchase flow (monthly or annual, and whether it went through), restoring a purchase — plus turning sample data on or off (and from where), switching the history range, the result of the first notification-permission prompt (allowed or not), changes to notification and display settings (which setting and which option), the number and type of AI tools connected along with whether their data could be read (including why not, and whether values are estimated), and the number of days until data first arrives from your Mac.

In addition, as standard behavior of the analytics service, application lifecycle events (launch, install, update, moving to the background) and your device model, OS version, app version and build number, language, screen size, connection type (Wi-Fi or cellular) and whether it is a TestFlight build are sent. Location lookup from your IP address is disabled.

We never send usage numbers (usage rate or token counts), reset times, your Mac’s name, file paths, project names, or anything related to your conversations or code. By design, the App’s analytics events cannot carry arbitrary runtime strings, so these are structurally impossible to send. We also never send personal information such as your name or email address.

Each record sent carries an anonymous identifier created for that installation of the App. It exists so that actions from the same installation can be read as one thread. It is not tied to your name, email address, account, or any hardware identifier provided by the device, and we do not use it to identify you. Deleting and reinstalling the App produces a new one.

We have no way to connect that identifier to you as a person. For that reason we cannot act on requests to retrieve or delete one user’s data individually. If you wish to stop the measurement entirely, please delete the App; nothing is sent after it is removed. The data is stored in the EU region.

The Mac app performs no analytics of any kind.

The App does not serve advertising or perform advertising tracking (ATT, IDFA, and similar) of any kind. Nor do we use the information collected for advertising purposes, or provide or sell it to third parties.

8. Purchases (RevenueCat)

Purchases and restores of the iPhone app’s paid features (AINOKORI Pro) are handled through Apple’s App Store, and we use the subscription infrastructure service RevenueCat to manage purchases and entitlement status. Information such as your purchase status, OS and app version, language and App Store country or region is therefore processed by RevenueCat. Payment details such as credit card numbers are handled by Apple and are never received by us. Purchases are managed using an anonymous identifier created for each installation of the App and your device’s identifier for vendor (IDFV — a value shared only among apps from the same developer, not the advertising identifier IDFA); both are sent to RevenueCat. Neither is linked to personal information such as your name or email address, and neither is used for tracking. Please note that RevenueCat is a service provided by a company based in the United States. The Mac app and Apple Watch app contain no purchasing mechanism.

9. Provision to third parties

We do not provide or sell your information to third parties for advertising purposes. Information sent to the external services described in sections 7 and 8 (PostHog and RevenueCat) for measurement and purchase processing is handled under each service’s own privacy policy.

Retention and deletion of information sent to PostHog and RevenueCat follow each company’s privacy policy (PostHog: https://posthog.com/privacy / RevenueCat: https://www.revenuecat.com/privacy).

10. Deleting your data

History stored on your iPhone can be erased from “Settings > Reset > Delete all history” in the app. Usage numbers stored in iCloud can be erased from “Settings > iCloud > Delete Data on iCloud…” in the Mac app. While the Mac app is running, the values are recreated on its next reading; to stop sending them, quit or delete the Mac app first. Numbers received from Claude Code can be erased with “Delete Records” in “How to Set Up…” (or “View Status…”) under “Settings > Tools > Claude Code” in the Mac app. To erase everything on your iPhone and Apple Watch, delete the app itself. macOS may keep the Mac app’s storage (~/Library/Containers/com.koheimitsui.AINokoriMac) after you delete the Mac app.

If you added the status-line setting to Claude Code, remove that setting (the statusLine entry you pasted) from ~/.claude/settings.json to stop receiving readings. Quitting or deleting the Mac app does not stop that setting; while the Mac app’s storage remains, Claude Code keeps writing to it.

Without your Mac, you can delete the iCloud data from your iPhone: Settings > [your name] > iCloud > Manage Account Storage > AINOKORI (the data is recreated if the Mac app is still running).

The App is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children.
