What makes the month easier to read

  • Events as labels

    Event titles appear as colored labels in the date grid. An event that spans several days becomes a single bar. When a day runs out of room, it shows how many are left, like “+3”. How many fit depends on your screen size.

  • 20 label colors

    Pick a label color for each event from 20 colors. The color you pick lives only in Koyomaru; it doesn’t change how other calendar apps show the event.

  • Tap for details

    Tap a label to open that event’s details, or tap a date to see that day’s list. From the details you can edit, duplicate, or delete the event.

  • Just the fields you need

    Title, color, all-day, start and end, repeat, alert, and notes. Add an event by touching and holding a date or with the + at the bottom right. Titles you’ve entered before come up as suggestions. Saved events go into your iPhone calendar, so other calendar apps show them too.

  • Search and jump

    Search events by title (from one year back to two years ahead). Tap the month and year to pick a date and jump to it. Swipe left or right for the next or previous month, and tap Today to come back to this month.

  • Holidays, birthdays, week numbers

    If your iPhone has a holiday calendar, the entries in that calendar show up as red labels. Birthdays can be shown or hidden in Settings. Week numbers (such as “W46”) run down the left edge.

  • Make it yours

    Week Starts On (Sunday or Monday), Appearance (Auto, Light, Dark), Expand Busy Weeks, and Button Position (Right or Left).

  • Lock and import

    Lock the app with Face ID, Touch ID, or your passcode. While locked, your events stay hidden in the app switcher, too. Events from another calendar app can be imported from an .ics file.

Koyomaru never sends your event details anywhere.

What you see are the events in your iPhone calendars. There’s no Koyomaru account or server that holds your events. Koyomaru never sends event titles, dates and times, or notes anywhere.

It does connect to outside services for anonymous usage analytics that help improve the app (PostHog) and for checking purchase status (RevenueCat), but what it sends is the kind of action, the option chosen, an anonymous identifier, and technical information such as the app and OS version — never your event details. Syncing with accounts such as iCloud or Google is done by your iPhone’s Calendar, not by Koyomaru. For details, see the Privacy Policy.
